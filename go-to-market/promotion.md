# How to promote Pith

Pith is a simple Mac meeting notepad for professionals who want the notes and do not want another system in their work. The first year is word of mouth. A good result is about **100 paying users**, which usually means a few thousand people tried the app. There is no ad budget, and none is required for that result.

Posting on every network at once spends the time that should go to those first hundred people. Be present where a working professional already is: in a personal note, on LinkedIn, and in an email letter they can keep. Launch in public once, after the Mac app actually installs.

## What to use

Do these in order.

1. **People you already know.** Make a list of 30 professionals who take meetings on a Mac: consultants, founders, lawyers, accountants, managers. Send each one a personal note and ask them to record one real meeting. Reply to every response yourself. This is the whole first month. Then ask who else on their team takes notes.
2. **LinkedIn, from your personal profile.** This is the buyer. Two posts a week, in English, and some in Spanish. Show one meeting turned into a short note. Skip the company page. Company pages get little reach.
3. **One email letter you own.** A short Substack, or the list on the Pith site, is enough. Write for the same reader: a professional who wants the notes and does not want another system. Medium publishes the same essay into a place you do not own, so keep one home.
4. **Product Hunt, once.** Do it only after **Download for Mac** installs a real app. That day can bring the first 50–200 signups. It will not carry the year. Tell the 30 users the date and ask them to try it that morning. A Tuesday is the usual day.
5. **Reddit, as a builder.** In Mac and productivity communities, ask people to tear the app apart. A straight promotional post gets removed and convinces nobody.

## One demo, reused

Record one 45-second screen video of a meeting becoming a note. Put that same video on LinkedIn, X, and, if you enjoy it, TikTok or Instagram Reels. Do not run those accounts as separate jobs.

## What waits

| Channel     | Why it waits                                                                                                                |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Kickstarter | The product and Stripe already exist. Backers are not subscribers, and the fee buys a launch format this app does not need. |
| Facebook    | The buyer is not discovering a Mac app there, and unpaid posts barely travel.                                               |
| Instagram   | Fine as a place to drop the demo, not as a focus.                                                                           |
| TikTok      | The demo can travel. The people who need meeting notes are mostly elsewhere.                                                |
| X / Twitter | Post the LinkedIn pieces here too. Expect conversation more than installs.                                                  |
| Telegram    | Useful only inside a professional group you already belong to. Cold posts get deleted.                                      |
| Paid ads    | Wait until 100 people are paying and you know which sentence made them install.                                             |

## Ninety days

- **Days 1–30.** The list of 30. Fix what they hit in the first meeting. Ask each one who else takes notes.
- **Days 31–60.** LinkedIn twice a week, plus the email letter twice a month. Spanish is the opening other meeting apps leave open: professionals in Mexico, Spain, Colombia, and Argentina who want the simple version in their language.
- **Days 61–90.** Product Hunt on a Tuesday, after the installer works. Spend that week answering comments. Do not start new channels that week.

## What a year looks like

A quiet month, with no launch, is often under 20 new people, and only a few of them pay. A launch month can be 50–200 signups and 5–20 paid. After that, 10–30 new signups a month is a normal solo pace.

One thousand people trying Pith in a year is a fair hope if the launch is public. One thousand paying users in year one is a breakout, not the plan. At $9.99, 100 paying users is about $12,000 a year before costs.
